# WORK SUMMARY 1 — Troubleshooting Suite (Panacea)

## PART I

### Goal

Panacea is an extensible, event-driven framework for automated observability and troubleshooting of Amazon Devices and Services at scale. It proactively detects problems through continuous signal monitoring, triggers autonomous diagnostic and remediation workflows powered by ML models, and uses specialized AI agents to diagnose symptoms, pinpoint root causes, and execute recovery — whether generating code fixes, pausing OTAs, or recommending rollbacks. The system learns from every issue it resolves, building shared knowledge that accelerates future diagnosis, and domain experts extend it by bringing their own data sources, detection models, and diagnosis workflows. Panacea was a 2025 DSLT goal (Kingpin: 930792) characterized by high domain, technical, and execution ambiguity — no prior platform existed to unify issue detection, diagnosis, and remediation across Amazon's device portfolio, and the engineering capabilities required spanned three independently-operating domains (Logs, Crashes, Search & Discovery) that had no shared delivery plan, shared milestones, or shared customer model. The role of the single-threaded leader on Panacea is to define what "unified troubleshooting" means for the Devices org, negotiate scope and sequencing across competing domain roadmaps, drive the underlying serverless architecture in partnership with Principal Engineering, and deliver a launchable platform that customer teams (EFD, Fire TV, Tablets, Device OS) actually adopt. Coming off the successful delivery of the Minerva Migration — a multi-year, cross-organizational program spanning 40+ teams and 8T metrics/day — my leadership identified me as the right person for this assignment: Panacea needed someone who had demonstrated the ability to stand up program structure from ambiguity, drive cross-domain alignment without formal authority, and deliver at DSLT scale. I was moved onto the program in May 2025, taking over the DSLT goal mid-flight with a 4–6 week transition and a hard 12/31/2025 delivery deadline. This gave me roughly seven months to build credibility with teams I had no prior relationship with, decompose an ambiguous problem space, establish a delivery structure that would survive the absence of a dedicated SDM on the team for the entirety of 2025, and ship a DSLT goal that the Devices Senior Leadership Team had bet on.

# WORK SUMMARY 2 — DS2 Metric Transport Convergence Strategy

## PART I

### Goal

Across DS2 and Devices product lines, the organization had accumulated eight independent metrics transport services — Minerva, Ceviche, FastMetrics, EIS, Mobilytics, Nexus, Vega Sushi Sender, and AWS IoT — each offering similar feature sets and creating significant metric duplication and operational overhead. The convergence work originated from a Monthly Customer Review (MCR) action item asking the org to determine a path to consolidating this fragmented landscape. A product assessment document was authored by the PMT that evaluated all eight services across data privacy handling, platform support, scale, delivery destinations, and transport latency/security, and concluded Minerva was the right convergence target — I supported the assessment by collecting technical details across the transport services and validating the gap analysis that identified near real-time delivery, Andes/SNS destinations, and OS platform coverage as the roadmap items required to absorb the other transports' customers. The convergence rationale operated on five dimensions: (1) operational burden of maintaining eight separate transport systems with similar AWS tech stacks, architectures, and approaches; (2) privacy risk from inconsistent handling — some systems (Ceviche, Vega Sushi Sender) had no automated privacy controls at all; (3) device performance impact from multiple transports collecting data independently and increasing memory, storage, and processing load on customer devices; (4) scale inefficiency from teams building bespoke infrastructure rather than leveraging a shared platform; and (5) growing regulatory pressure (GDPR, CCPA, evolving privacy laws) requiring consistent, auditable data handling that fragmented systems could not guarantee. My role was to take the assessment's conclusion and turn it into an executable convergence strategy: frame the problem at the VP level (not as a set of independent migrations, but as one strategic simplification of Devices metric infrastructure), author the technical convergence path that reconciled fundamentally different aggregation models, and broker L8-level alignment across competing organizational priorities. VCA-to-Minerva (Kingpin: 957146) and Ceviche-to-Minerva were the two major convergence programs following the product assessment, and I drove both as the single-threaded leader — planning the strategy, driving alignment, and executing the convergence path.

## PART II: Results

### Quality of Work / Challenge

The convergence effort required reframing what looked like a set of operational migrations into a single VP-level strategic question: should the Devices org carry eight metric-transport services indefinitely, or converge to one? With the product assessment establishing Minerva as the convergence target, I authored the VCA-to-Minerva Convergence Path document that did three things the prior conversation had not done. First, it surfaced the actual technical incompatibility between the two systems with specificity — VCA's hourly persistent aggregation versus Minerva's app-controlled model is not a "configuration difference," it's an architectural one — and it mapped the feature gap rigorously: statistical min/max, histogram-based aggregation, and pre-aggregated data handling were the minimal set of capabilities Minerva needed to absorb VCA's customers without breaking their workflows. Second, it scoped that delta as an engineering plan with phased delivery, which converted "this convergence is too hard" from an opinion into a quantified set of work items Minerva could commit to. Third, it preserved VCA's aggregation semantics within Minerva's architecture, which gave the VCA team an honest path to convergence rather than a top-down mandate that would have invited resistance. The scale being reconciled is non-trivial: VCA's KDM pipeline handles approximately 890 billion events per day aggregated from 69 billion raw metrics, serving every device health team (Performance, Stability, eMMC, Connectivity) across FOS, ACS-NoART, and Vega — a customer base that cannot tolerate gaps in metric continuity during a transition. I drove the L8 review in partnership with the SPOC on the VCA Device OS team and obtained sign-off on 5/7/2025 — which unlocked the follow-on engineering: enhanced on-device service-side aggregation in Minerva (statistical min/max, histogram-based aggregation, pre-aggregated data handling — feature complete by 5/19/2025) and the Minerva RTOS client adoption path for ACK 3P devices in partnership with Device OS and Amazon Connected Kit. In parallel, I drove the Ceviche-to-Minerva convergence to L8-level goal alignment across both the Minerva and Ceviche organizations, defining scope and success criteria and standing up a dedicated Launch Readiness tracker to make the parallel migration legible to leadership. Once that goal alignment was secured, I handed execution to the SDMs on both the Minerva and Ceviche teams and was brought onto Panacea as the next L7-scope assignment. The decision-quality bar on this work was high because convergence decisions are durable: a wrong call on aggregation semantics would have either broken Crash's stateless-safe path or forced VCA's customers into a multi-year operational regression. The work was technically grounded (aggregation-model analysis, KDM pipeline characterization) and organizationally grounded (L8 stakeholder alignment with competing investments), and the convergence document is now the technical blueprint for unifying metric transport across the Devices org.

The VCA-to-Minerva convergence goal (Kingpin 957146) received L8 sign-off on 5/7/2025, directly supporting VP-level goals for simplifying Devices metric infrastructure. # WORK SUMMARY 3 — Minerva Migration & DCM Deprecation

## PART I

### Goal

The Minerva Migration program was a multi-year, cross-organizational initiative to migrate all Amazon device and application teams from the legacy Device Client Metrics (DCM) transport service to Minerva, the next-generation metric transport platform — and subsequently deprecate DCM clients across all product lines. The scope is the scale: DCM transported 8 trillion metrics per day across 69,000 unique programs spanning 50+ named programs, 5+ product lines (Fire TV, EFD, Tablets, eReaders), and multiple OS platforms (iOS, Android, FOS, Vega, RTOS, ACS), with mShop and PrimeVideo as major application customers and a top-100-program tail accounting for 92% of volume. The structural problem underneath the scale was the absence of a metric-ownership model in DCM: there was no concept of who owned what, which made it impossible to deprecate metrics safely without a discovery-and-claim mechanism, and which made the long tail of 68K programs (the remaining 7.5% of volume) operationally radioactive. The role of the TPM on Minerva was to author the migration strategy and deprecation plan, build the tracking and self-service mechanisms that made migration possible at scale, drive 40+ teams across multiple VP organizations to actually migrate, and complete the deprecation phase without customer-facing metric regression. I joined Amazon in May 2021 and was immediately tasked with this program at a time when the DCM team had no SDM in place, which expanded the TPM role into running the engineering operating cadence with SDEs building the Minerva clients (FOS, Android, ACS/Vega, iOS, RTOS) in parallel with authoring the migration strategy for 50+ programs across the org.

## PART II: Results

### Quality of Work / Challenge

Minerva Migration was a four-year program in which the hard problem evolved every year — from establishing the migration strategy and building the Minerva clients in 2021–2022, to driving 40+ teams through migration in 2023, to deprecating DCM clients across all product lines in 2024, to managing the residual long tail of devices that cannot receive OTA software updates in 2025. I authored the three foundational documents that defined the program's operating model: the Minerva Strategy document (phased migration with 60% completion by 12/31/2022 and remainder by 6/30/2023), the DCM Deprecation Plan (server-side and client-side deprecation strategy spanning block-and-store, remote blocklisting, bloom-filter allowlisting, stub/no-op clients, and server-side forwarding for legacy devices), and the "Where is my DCM Metric?" product requirements (the self-service discovery tool that let teams find and claim their metrics to prevent accidental deprecation — the mechanism that made the long tail tractable). Against that strategic backbone I built the operational mechanisms: a top-100-program tracker covering 92% of volume, a metrics tracking dashboard for monitoring volume reduction per program, a bi-weekly migration review used by L8+ leaders for triage and decision-making, and a migration issue tracker for cross-team blockers. The hardest part of the migration was not moving teams off DCM — it was delivering Minerva at feature parity and beyond so customers achieved the same business outcomes (and more) on the new transport. I partnered with the Minerva engineering team to validate device latency metrics between DMETs and Minerva outputs, drove root-cause analysis across device teams when reporting diverged, and tracked down cross-team issues that were critical to closing parity gaps on device reporting. When standard release vehicles were insufficient to meet the migration timeline, I negotiated additional system OTAs — a non-trivial ask that required building a compelling case to OS leadership weighing the cost of delay against the risk of incremental OTA pushes. During the FOS7 Tablets DEM cutover I identified Minerva metric loss on 2.9% of actively-used devices, drove RCA across three contributing factors (Game Mode, Arcus crash, System Server crash), and coordinated hotfix delivery — the kind of cross-team integration problem the program owner has to chase down rather than triage. The deprecation phase required managing the long tail with discipline: I established clear deprecation criteria, implemented the block-and-store mechanism for unclaimed metrics, built the server-side forwarding solution for legacy devices that could not receive OTAs, and escalated blockers through the appropriate leadership channels — including driving the Connectivity DCM Deprecation to closure in Q2 2024 by resolving stalled cross-team blockers (DMETS-11543, DMETS-11619) that had been slipping week-over-week until I intervened. Throughout the 4-year journey I operated at the technical depth a Principal TPM is expected to operate at on a platform program. With no SDM on the team at program start, I ran the engineering operating cadence myself; when a new SDM joined I coached them into the role and served as the program's continuity through the transition; when that SDM later moved on, I helped a Sr. SDE grow into the SDM role through interim and into the permanent position. The migration's continuity through multiple SDM transitions, two product-line OS generations, and a complete deprecation of the legacy transport is the evidence of sustained ownership at L7 scope.

### Impact / Measure of Success

Across the four-year arc, Minerva Migration and DCM Deprecation delivered measurable outcomes against every dimension of the program. On migration: 40+ Devices and Apps teams migrated from DCM to Minerva, delivering a DSLT goal. On volume: monthly metrics volume reduced from 295 trillion to 99.38 trillion — a 195.89-trillion-metric reduction (66.34%), exceeding the 60% target set for 2023. On cost: Metrics-service AWS cost reduced from a projected $13.5M to $9.43M, delivering $4.07M in IMR cost savings (30%) — three times the 10% target. On 2024 deprecation: weekly DCM traffic dropped from 18.4 trillion (January 2024) to 2.6 trillion (November 2024) — an 86% reduction — through continued metric migration, OTA saturation, and gradual client-side traffic blocking of Clickstream metrics; the residual ~2.1 trillion weekly traffic comes from devices and apps that will not receive OTA software updates, the irreducible tail the strategy planned for. On the mShop migration: of 30 tracked teams, 24 completed migration, 6 were deprecated, 1 confirmed alignment — with zero customer-facing metric loss, completed January 2025. On product-line completion: DCM client deprecation was driven to completion across all product lines including Vega and NoART devices. On the mechanism side: the bi-weekly Minerva Migration review became the L8+ decision-making forum for the program, and the "Where is my DCM Metric?" self-service tool eliminated the discovery bottleneck that would otherwise have made the long-tail migration impossible. The program reduced operational overhead across the Devices metric pipeline, eliminated divergent metric pipelines that had created reporting inconsistencies, and set up the convergence work (see Work Summary on Metric Transport Convergence) that consolidated Ceviche and VCA onto Minerva as the unified transport.

### Leadership Skill & Influence

The Minerva Migration required influencing 40+ teams that did not report to me, across multiple VP organizations, with competing priorities and no formal authority for migration ordering. I built consensus through data — identifying the top programs by metric volume and customer impact, presenting clear ROI for each team's migration effort, and quantifying the cost of delay (continued DCM maintenance burden, divergent metric pipelines, blocked downstream features) when teams pushed back on timelines. I prepared and ran the bi-weekly Minerva Migration risk review with L10 and L8 leaders as the program's decision-making forum, which gave leadership the visibility to apply top-down support where needed and gave my partner teams a predictable surface for triage. The leadership development dimension of this program is the part that maps most directly to the Principal TPM Role Guideline expectation of significant role in career development of others: with no SDM on the DCM team at program start, I ran the engineering operating cadence myself for almost a year, then coached two SDMs through the program — one ramped into the role from a new hire, one grown from a Sr. SDE into interim and then permanent SDM — with program continuity preserved through both transitions. The combination — multi-year sustained ownership of a DSLT-scale program, technical depth on data integrity validation, organizational influence across 40+ teams and multiple VP orgs, and direct role in developing two SDMs through the lifecycle — is the L7 signal on this work.

## Panorama — Customer Journey Analytics Platform (MLP & Launch)

From 2021–2022, I drove the end-to-end delivery of Panorama — Amazon's first unified customer journey analytics platform for Devices — from MLP through full production launch. Panorama stitched together device engagement metrics (DEM: power on, user present, app usage), Out-of-Box Experience (OOBE) telemetry, navigation data, crash data, and Early Device Engagement (EDE) metrics into a single, session-aware clickstream dataset spanning Fire TV, Tablets, and eReaders — enabling cross-product-line customer journey analysis that had never been possible before.

**MLP (delivered 12/28/2021):** I drove the MLP to completion on schedule, delivering: (1) a standardized ADC schema for collecting and aggregating data across product lines, (2) device and customer session IDs applied to Panorama datasets, (3) onboarding of Tablet and eReader OOBE metrics, and (4) a new suite of Early Device Engagement metrics including improved FRO and First Network Connection (FNC). Prior to MLP, I conducted customer journey interviews (weeks of 9/6 and 9/13/2021) across product-line stakeholders to understand pain points and use cases — findings that shaped the Panorama 2022 Roadmap and 3-Year Vision (published 12/17/2021).

**Post-MLP through Launch (Q1–Q3 2022):** Following MLP feedback review (1/7/2022), I drove the platform to full production: established the data ingestion process for partner teams, built the scalable framework for data access control and partitioning, and delivered standardized MLP dashboards by 2/28/2022 supporting cross-product-line journey analysis, engagement gap identification, and session navigation path profiling. I then drove the dataset onboarding roadmap to completion:
- FireTV Navigation — onboarded 6/30/2022
- Tablet Navigation — onboarded 6/30/2022
- FireTV OOBE — onboarded 7/15/2022
- FireTV and Tablet Crash data — onboarded 9/26/2022

**Goal completion (9/26/2022):** Delivered the centralized Devices Clickstream solution (Panorama) by onboarding DEM, crashes, FireTV and Tablet OOBE, and navigation data — with Panorama Dashboards supporting 8 customer journey use cases in production.

The program spanned 20 milestones across 8 workstreams — including infrastructure (schema, intake mechanisms, access controls, data warehousing, data vending, quality scoring), EDE metric improvements (FNC, First App Open, First Customer Activation), and product-line onboarding — each with phased task breakdowns I tracked through delivery. I also authored the PRFAQ for troubleshooting improvements leveraging Panorama data — which seeded the vision that later became the Panacea Troubleshooting Suite (see Work Summary 1). This work demonstrates the ability to define and deliver a net-new data platform from zero, drive cross-team data integration at scale, conduct customer discovery to shape product direction, and establish governance and operating mechanisms that outlast the initial delivery.
